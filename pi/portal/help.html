How to use the Ark

One page, plain words. If you can use a website, you can use this.

Home How to use

What this box is

The Ark is a small computer that carries a library. It makes its own Wi-Fi network, so phones, tablets and laptops can read everything on it without any internet at all. Reading, playing and sharing need no account. (The owner has a private sign-in for looking after the box, and a game save can have a name of its own; neither is needed to read or play.)

If a “Sign in to Wi-Fi” window opens

That window is the phone trying to be helpful. It is not a real sign-in page and you do not need an account. Two things to know:

  • Some buttons inside that window do nothing. That is normal. Close the window and open a normal browser instead.
  • Type ark.lan into the address bar. The full address is http://ark.lan — the “http://” part matters.
Two addresses always work: http://ark.lan and http://10.42.0.1. On a computer joined to an ordinary home Wi-Fi, use http://ark.local instead — that is the name a computer can look up by itself. If one does not open, try the other.

What is inside

  • Offline Library Encyclopedias, medical references and question-and-answer archives. Pick a collection, then use its search box.
  • Books Full-length books. Tap a cover to read it in the browser or to download it.
  • Manuals & Guides Short pages written for a phone screen: water, first aid, power, plumbing, wiring.
  • Pocket Cards to Print One page each. Print them and tape them inside a cupboard door.
  • Message Board Leave a note for the next person. Anyone on the network can read and post.
  • File Box Share a file with everyone. Tap “Choose a file”, then “Put it in the box”. A film can go here too (up to 8 GB) — it is kept for the owner to look at before it appears on the Films shelf.
  • Films Films kept on this box. Tap a picture to watch in your browser; the player remembers where you stopped in this browser, and the small “Start over” button takes you back to the beginning.
  • Maps Download one region to your phone while you are on the Ark. Afterwards the map, the place search and new directions work with no Ark and no internet. The first time, the phone has to trust the Ark’s certificate — the Maps page and its “How this works” page walk you through it.
  • Games Small games that run right here. Tap one and play; no name is needed.
  • This Household Who lives here, where the water and the power stop, who to ring in an emergency — and which emergency pages have been read lately.
  • Card to Get Back On A card to print with a square a phone camera can read, so a visitor can find the box again.

Reading on a phone

Everything is built for a small screen: big buttons, dark background so it does not glare at night, and text you can read without zooming. Pages you open in the Offline Library have their own back button — use your phone’s back arrow to come back here.

Taking maps with you

Open Maps from the front page. Choose a region and tap Download for offline use. Wait until it says Ready offline — that only appears after every part has arrived and been checked. Then you can leave the Ark’s Wi-Fi and open Maps from the home-screen icon or bookmark: browse, search a place and calculate a new route, all on the phone.

The first time on each phone, the phone must trust the Ark’s own certificate, and the page must be opened at its secure address (https://ark.local:8446/maps/). The certificate is offered on the front page as the Ark’s certificate; the Maps help page has the exact steps for iPhone, Android and a laptop. Without that one-time step the map still works while the Ark is reachable, but it cannot reopen with the Ark gone.

Opening the Maps page does not download a region by itself. Directions use the speed of each kind of road, not live traffic; mapped water is not a promise that it is drinkable; and street addresses are usually not in a region. There is more detail under How this works on the Maps page itself.

Keeping it working

  • Leave the box plugged in. It draws about as much power as a phone charger.
  • The Wi-Fi has no password on purpose. Anyone nearby can read the library — that is the point. Do not keep secrets in the File Box.
  • Big collections keep arriving on their own when the box finds an internet connection. You do not have to do anything; new material simply appears.

Still stuck? Try http://10.42.0.1 instead of ark.lan, and make sure you are joined to the network called The-Ark-Offline.
